# M2 backtest: `candle_close_confirmation` breakout against historical SIP quotes

**Dataset:** `reports/datasets/m2_sip_20260603_20261009/` (90 trading days; see `reports/m2_data_readiness.md`). The committed outputs are the aggregate `backtest_summary_in_sample.json` and `backtest_summary_out_of_sample.json`. Per-trade rows and raw quotes are derived from licensed data and stay local under `backtest/` and `cache/backtest_quotes/`.
**Code:** `1b76f5c` (corrected execution model) for both runs; the superseded pre-fix runs used `7f89c17` · **API:** Alpaca historical market data only. The corrected reruns made 0 quote requests in-sample and 13 out-of-sample, reusing the local cache (the pre-fix runs made 1,608 and 906) · **Orders placed:** 0. There is no live or paper order path anywhere in the code.
**Samples:** in-sample is 59 trading days, 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-26 (3,159 signals). One embargo day (2026-08-27) is excluded. Out-of-sample is 30 trading days, 2026-08-28 to 2026-10-09 (1,488 signals).

## Strategy definition: `candle_close_confirmation`

The strategy tested here is **`candle_close_confirmation`**: a breakout counts only after the 1-minute trigger bar has **closed** above the earlier session high, and the simulated order is sent when that completed bar becomes known (bar end + latency). It never enters during the bar.

The label is recorded in code as `StrategyConfig.entry_trigger`. It was added after the reruns, so the committed summaries (generated by `1b76f5c`) do not list it yet; it is a name only and does not change any result.

An **intrabar resistance-cross entry**, buying as soon as price trades through resistance before the candle closes, is a different strategy with different fills, false-break rate and costs. It is not evaluated here, and none of these results apply to it. It would need its own pre-registered hypothesis, trade-level (not bar-level) data and a fresh hold-out.

## Execution-model correction (rerun complete)

Both samples were rerun with the corrected model in `1b76f5c`, using the existing cached SIP quotes. Strategy, risk settings and the frozen 50 bps cap are unchanged. The corrected in-sample rerun still selects 50 bps under the pre-registered rule, so the out-of-sample rerun used the same frozen configuration. It is a model-correction evaluation, not a parameter search; nothing was tuned on it.

What changed in the model:

1. **Incomplete exits were counted as realized P&L.** When an exit did not fully fill, the unsold shares were valued at the last executable bid, including bids after the exit deadline. That value was added to proceeds and reported through `net_usd` and `r_multiple`. Now:
   - The exit order lives from trigger + latency to the end of its exit window: trigger + 2 minutes (`EXIT_SLACK`), never past the session close. Quotes after that time are not used for fills or valuation.
   - Realized proceeds, gross and fees cover only shares actually sold (`exit_qty`). Unsold shares (`remaining_qty`) are marked at the last executable bid at or before the window end (`mark_price`, `unrealized_usd`) and are never realized.
   - `net_usd` and `r_multiple` are set only for completed exits. Summary P&L, win rate, expectancy and the spread-cap selection count completed exits only (`trades`); `entries` counts all filled entries, and `incomplete_exits` reports count, shares unsold, realized and marked value separately.
   - A position with unsold shares stays open: no new entry for the rest of that session, and its realized plus marked loss counts against the daily limits (the full entry value if it cannot be marked).
2. **Displayed NBBO size was re-used.** Each quote update offered its full displayed size again, so an unchanged quote repeated three times could fill three times its size. Now an order may take at most displayed size × size share at a price level, minus what it already took at that level. Only a larger size at the same price (evidence of replenishment) or a new price level adds liquidity. Unfilled entry quantity still expires after the 10 s timeout.

- **Conclusions unchanged.** Every realistic configuration is still clearly negative. Base at 50 bps moved by less than 0.01 R in both samples; the 50 bps cap is still selected.

## Result

**The strategy has no edge on this data.** Every configuration has negative average R, both in-sample and out-of-sample. Even the idealized upper bound (no latency, mid-price fills, unlimited size, no fees) is about −0.04 R in-sample and −0.06 R out-of-sample, and its confidence interval includes zero. Realistic execution makes it clearly negative.

## Method

- **Signal:** a candidate-gated SIP session-high breakout, using the M1 scanner unchanged:
  - price $1–$20;
  - change vs the previous close ≥ 10% premarket, ≥ 5% after hours;
  - session volume ≥ 100k;
  - breakout-bar volume ≥ 2× the mean of the previous five completed bars.

  The signal becomes available when its 1-minute bar completes (`*_signal_available_utc`). Nothing earlier is used.
- **Entry:** a marketable buy limit at the signal-time ask + 20 bps.
  - The quote at signal time must be fresh (≤ 3 s old), executable (ask > bid) and within the spread cap.
  - The order arrives after latency. It fills only on fresh executable quotes, up to the displayed ask size not already taken at that price (corrected model; see above).
  - Any unfilled remainder expires after 10 s.
  - A candle touching a price never counts as a fill.
- **Exit:** stop at −3%, target at +2R (+6%), or a 15-minute time stop, flat 2 minutes before the session ends.
  - Exits trigger only on fresh executable bids.
  - They sell at the bid after latency, up to the displayed bid size not already taken at that price, until filled or until the exit window ends (corrected model: trigger + 2 minutes, at most the session close). Unsold shares are reported as an incomplete exit, not as realized P&L.
- **Risk** (docs/DESIGN.md section 5):
  - $3,000 account, $15 risk per trade, maximum notional 25%;
  - one position at a time, 5 entries per day;
  - $45 daily loss limit, pause after 3 consecutive losses;
  - no new entries in the last 10 minutes of a session.
- **Costs:** the spread (fills at the touch), SEC fee $27.80 per $1M sold, FINRA TAF $0.000166 per share sold (maximum $8.30), and zero commission.
  - These fee rates are constants in code; check them against current published rates.
  - Fees total $4–11 per run, small next to spread costs.
- **Fixed settings:** strategy and risk settings (`StrategyConfig`, `RiskConfig`) were fixed before any run. Only the spread cap was compared, on in-sample data.

## Spread-cap selection (in-sample only)

The selection rule was set in code before the run (`select_spread_cap`). It picks the highest base-case average R among caps with at least 30 trades; ties go to the tighter cap.
- **Which caps qualified:** all four (229–236 completed trades each).
- **Selected cap:** **50 bps**, the least negative at −0.236 R (unchanged by the model correction).

The 50 bps cap was then frozen, and out-of-sample was run once with it. Nothing was retuned afterwards.

Wider caps fill more signals but do not add trades, because the daily limits bind. Instead they swap tight-spread entries for wide-spread ones, and average R falls at each step: −0.24, −0.36, −0.38, −0.39 R in the base case.

## Quote coverage

- **Quote availability:** historical SIP quotes were present at signal time for 94% of evaluated signals in both samples. A missing quote within the 3 s before the signal (5.8% in-sample, 54 of 925; 6.3% out-of-sample, 28 of 442, base at 50 bps) is recorded as a non-fill. A fill is never synthesized from bars.
- **Locked quotes:** a locked quote at signal time (1.3% and 2.0%) is a non-fill.
- **Wide spreads:** this is the main rejection. At the 50 bps cap, 65% of evaluated in-sample signals and 64% out-of-sample were refused at signal time for spread above the cap.
- **Incomplete exits:** none in-sample. Out-of-sample there was 1 in base and 1 in stress, the same trade: its time exit sold no shares within the 2-minute exit window. Its 45 shares are marked at the last executable bid in the window ($0.00 base, −$0.99 stress) and excluded from P&L. Under `7f89c17` the mark (−$0.02 and −$1.01, using bids after the deadline) was counted as realized.

Quote coverage is therefore not what limits the result. The wide spreads are.

## After hours is barely tested

The daily limits are shared across the whole trading day, and premarket comes first. The 5 entries, the $45 loss limit or the 3-loss pause are usually used up before 16:00 ET.
- **In-sample, base at 50 bps:** 1,409 of 1,537 after-hours signals were skipped for this reason, leaving 22 after-hours trades.
- **Idealized:** no after-hours trades at all, because its higher premarket fill rate uses up the limits sooner.

After-hours results (12–22 trades) are too few to judge. The engine follows the design's risk policy as written. Evaluating after hours separately would need a separate risk budget, which is a decision for review.

## Assumptions that may overstate execution quality

1. **Displayed size is ours to take.** The corrected model no longer re-uses the same displayed shares, but it still assumes no competing orders take them first and that a size increase is new liquidity available to us. Historical Level 1 NBBO cannot prove an order would have filled: it shows no queue position, hidden or odd-lot liquidity, or who traded the displayed size. (In the pre-fix `7f89c17` runs, consecutive updates at the same price re-offered the same shares.)
2. **Short latency.** The base case acts 0.5 s after the bar ends. A live system must first receive the bar, which arrives after the minute closes. It must then scan thousands of symbols and route the order. The stress case's 2 s is probably closer to reality.
3. **Stops fill at the bid prevailing when the order arrives.** In fast drops, a real stop can fill well below the bid it triggered on, and the bid-size limit only partly captures this.
4. **No halts, venue-specific extended-hours rules or broker rejections.** Alpaca is assumed to accept extended-hours limit orders from 04:00 ET.
5. **Survivorship bias** in the universe (see the data readiness report) most likely leans optimistic.
6. **Out-of-sample contamination.** The out-of-sample block was inspected for data quality in M1-B. The quote probe's spread statistics, which framed the cap grid, included 19 out-of-sample windows. No strategy parameter was fitted on out-of-sample trades.

Assumptions that understate execution quality: no price improvement, no hidden or midpoint liquidity, odd lots ignored (the SIP NBBO is round lots), and locked quotes never fill.

## Reproduce

The out-of-sample command reruns the same frozen configuration. After the model correction it was run once more as a model-correction evaluation, not a new parameter search.

```bash
momentum-bot m1-dataset --start 2026-06-03 --end 2026-10-09 --out-dir reports/datasets/m2_sip_20260603_20261009
momentum-bot m2-backtest --dataset reports/datasets/m2_sip_20260603_20261009 --sample in_sample --spread-caps 50,100,150,200
# frozen configuration, run once:
momentum-bot m2-backtest --dataset reports/datasets/m2_sip_20260603_20261009 --sample out_of_sample --spread-caps 50 --confirm-out-of-sample
```

Reruns reuse the local quote cache. A fresh clone needs about 2,500 quote requests, roughly 45 minutes.

## Recommendation

Do not promote this setup to paper trading as a strategy. Its best realistic configuration loses about 0.2 R per trade after spreads, before and after the execution-model correction, and the idealized ceiling is about zero. The M3 paper infrastructure can still be built and tested for plumbing, but running it with this strategy should be expected to lose money. Decisions for review:

1. Accept that the initial breakout hypothesis failed on this data, and choose and pre-register the next one.
2. Decide whether after hours gets its own risk budget so it can be evaluated on its own.
3. Reserve trading days after 2026-10-09 as an untouched hold-out for the next hypothesis.
